This paper presents a technically interesting 14-DOF biped
robot with active toes and a high-fidelity
reinforcement-learning simulation framework. A major
strength is the realistic modeling of the coupled
transmission in motor space, including torque-speed limits,
joint friction, Joule heating, and mechanical power. The
comparison between the active-toe and toe-ablation models
is generally well designed, and the reported reductions in
CoT and path deviation indicate that active toes can
improve locomotion efficiency and directional control.

The paper is clearly written and presents a meaningful
technological contribution. I have several minor comments.

1. Please clarify the exact toe-ablation setup. Although
the action dimension is reduced from 14 to 12, it is
unclear whether the toe joint, actuator, link,
mass/inertia, and contact geometry are removed, locked, or
otherwise modified. This is important because active toe
actuation and altered foot-ground contact geometry may both
contribute to the reported improvements.

2. In the T-test, the toe-ablation model achieves higher
maximum and average speeds, while both models have nearly
identical completion times. The active-toe model clearly
provides better path accuracy, but the result also
indicates a speed-accuracy trade-off. The definition of
agility should therefore be clarified, and additional
metrics such as turning radius, lateral acceleration, or
yaw rate would strengthen the claim.

3. The simulation model is carefully constructed, but no
physical robot experiments are included. Even limited
hardware validation would substantially increase the
paper’s impact. Otherwise, the remaining effects of belt
compliance, backlash, and contact-model uncertainty should
be discussed more explicitly.

4. Please report standard deviations or confidence
intervals over the ten trials and clarify whether they
correspond to repeated rollouts of one policy or
independent training seeds.

Overall, this is a solid conference paper with a meaningful
hardware concept and a careful simulation methodology. The
comments above are primarily requests for clarification and
additional supporting analysis.
