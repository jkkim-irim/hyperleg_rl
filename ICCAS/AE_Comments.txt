Comments to author (Associate Editor)
=====================================

The proposed work extends the authors' active-toe HyperLeg
mechanism to a 14-DOF biped and compares toe-equipped and
toe-ablation configurations under a common
reinforcement-learning procedure with actuator- and
transmission-aware modeling. The reviewers agree that the
coupled-transmission treatment in motor space, the
energy-based formulation, and the clear multi-metric
comparison are meaningful strengths. The main concern is
that the agility claim exceeds what the evaluation
supports, since the toe-equipped model shows lower path
deviation but also lower speed, so the result is better
described as improved path-tracking under a possible
speed-accuracy trade-off; the authors are recommended to
temper this wording and to report one or two mechanistic
quantities, such as yaw-rate error or center-of-pressure
trajectory, that explain how a sagittal toe motion affects
turning. The high-fidelity and sim-to-real claims should
also be framed more precisely as actuator- and
transmission-aware simulation, since no hardware validation
is provided, and the toe-ablation setup should be specified
clearly. In addition, variability across the ten trials
should be reported with the number of training seeds, the
GRF and CoT definitions should be made consistent and
reproducible, and the noted notation, equation, and
reference issues corrected.
